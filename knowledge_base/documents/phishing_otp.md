# Phishing: fake messages that steal your banking details

Phishing is when a fraudster sends an email, SMS, WhatsApp message or social media message pretending to be from a bank, government agency, courier or well-known company. The message usually contains a link to a fake website that looks like the real one, or asks you to reply with details.

Common warning signs:
- Urgency or threats: "your account will be blocked", "KYC expires today", "update PAN within 24 hours".
- A link that does not match the official domain of the organisation, or uses a URL shortener.
- Requests for an OTP, card number, CVV, UPI PIN, net-banking password or login ID.
- Messages from personal mobile numbers or unofficial accounts instead of the bank's registered sender IDs.
- Spelling mistakes and generic greetings.

Banks and the RBI never ask customers for OTPs, PINs, passwords or CVV over phone, SMS, email or chat. An OTP authorises a transaction or account change — anyone who has it can use it.

If you clicked a link or entered details:
- Contact your bank immediately through official channels to block cards/net-banking and check for transactions.
- Change passwords and PINs for affected accounts and enable two-factor authentication.
- If money was lost, report it quickly to the national cyber fraud helpline and portal.
- Keep the message, sender number and link as evidence; do not delete them.
