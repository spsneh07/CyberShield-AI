# Romance scams

A fraudster builds an online relationship through dating apps, matrimonial sites or social media, often using stolen photos and a story of working abroad. After gaining trust they ask for money for an emergency, travel, medical bills, or "customs charges" to release a gift or parcel. Some move the conversation to video calls that are recorded and later used for blackmail.

Warning signs: the person avoids meeting or live video, professes strong feelings quickly, has a dramatic hardship story, and asks for money, gift cards or crypto. Genuine customs or courier services do not ask you to pay personal accounts or UPI IDs to release a gift.

# Cryptocurrency investment scams

Fraudsters promote crypto trading platforms, apps or "mentors" promising guaranteed or very high returns. Victims see fake profits on a dashboard but cannot withdraw unless they pay more as "tax", "fees" or "unlock charges". Some scams begin as a romance or friendship and later turn into an investment pitch.

What to do for both: stop paying — further payments never unlock money; do not delete chats; save profile links, usernames, wallet addresses, UPI IDs, bank details and transaction IDs; report the profile to the platform and report the fraud through the national cybercrime helpline or portal and your bank.
