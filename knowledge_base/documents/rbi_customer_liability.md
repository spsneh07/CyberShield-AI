# Unauthorised electronic banking transactions — what customers should know

The Reserve Bank of India issued directions (July 2017) on customer protection in unauthorised electronic banking transactions. Banks are required to send transaction alerts and to provide customers with ways (such as a phone number, SMS, email, website or app) to report unauthorised transactions at any time.

Key points of the framework, in simple terms:
- Report an unauthorised electronic transaction to your bank immediately. The sooner it is reported, the lower your liability.
- Where the fraud is due to the bank's deficiency, or a third-party breach that is neither the bank's nor the customer's fault and the customer reports it within three working days of receiving the bank's communication, the customer's liability can be zero.
- Where the loss is due to the customer's negligence, such as sharing payment credentials like an OTP or PIN, the customer may bear the loss until the transaction is reported to the bank.
- Delayed reporting (beyond three working days) can increase the customer's liability, as per the bank's board-approved policy.
- Banks are expected to resolve the complaint and establish liability within a defined time frame; check your bank's customer-protection policy for details.

Practical steps: keep SMS/email alerts enabled, register a valid mobile number with your bank, and report any unfamiliar transaction to the bank's official channels right away. Keep the complaint reference number given by the bank.
