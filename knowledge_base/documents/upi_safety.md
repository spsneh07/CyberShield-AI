# UPI safety

UPI (Unified Payments Interface) lets you send money instantly using a UPI ID (for example name@bank) or a QR code.

The most important rule: you never need to enter your UPI PIN to RECEIVE money. Entering your UPI PIN always authorises money to LEAVE your account. Fraudsters exploit this through:
- Collect requests disguised as "refunds", "prize money" or "payment from buyer" — approving them sends money to the fraudster.
- QR codes sent by "buyers" on marketplace sites — scanning and entering the PIN pays the fraudster.
- Fake customer-care numbers found through internet searches, who ask you to install screen-sharing apps or approve requests.

Safety tips:
- Read every request screen carefully: check whether it says you are paying.
- Never share your UPI PIN or OTP with anyone.
- Use only the official app and in-app help for support; do not search the internet for "customer care" numbers.
- Do not install remote-access or screen-sharing apps at the request of a caller.

If you were defrauded via UPI: raise a complaint in your UPI app (transaction > report issue), inform your bank immediately, report the fraud on the national cybercrime helpline/portal, and keep the transaction ID (UTR) and the fraudster's UPI ID.
